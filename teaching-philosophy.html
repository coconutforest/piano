Music is a universal language that connects people. To me, music study is important as it allows us to communicate emotions, messages, and stories in ways that words cannot. Studying music supports personal growth that extends beyond the piano. Students develop determination, creativity, focus, attention to details, and hard work that carry into their personal lives.

About Me

I began playing piano when I was five years old. I still remember watching my kindergarten teacher perform during school assemblies. I dreamt of one day playing just like her. Learning piano has been one of the most rewarding journeys in my life. Music study has taught me discipline, helped me discover many musical styles, and strengthened my confidence as I expanded my repertoire and reached new milestones.

I would like to share my passion for music with my students, so that they can appreciate music and express their emotions through music as well.

My Teaching Approach

I specialize in teaching piano from beginner levels through RCM Level 4 (Royal Conservatory of Music), working with students from five years old through adulthood. For beginners, I use a mixed-method approach that includes the Middle C approach, learning by listening, and understanding musical direction. One of the book series beginner students will use is Succeeding at the Piano by Helen Marlais.

My goal is to support students in building strong music literacy and foundational techniques so they can play expressively and learn music independently and confidently.

To support a well-rounded music study, I focus on developing the following skills:

  • Playing technique — Develop proper finger, hand, wrist, and arm control to produce a good quality tone at the piano.
  • Theory and music literacy — Learn music theory including pitches, rhythm, musical structure, and basic history.
  • Listening and reading of music — Gain music reading and aural skills so students can explore music more freely on their own.
  • Expression of music — Explore stories and moods within pieces through imagination and creative exercises.

The Importance of Parental Involvement

Parents play an important role in their child's musical development. Showing appreciation for your child's practice and offering encouragement make them feel proud of their progress. Parents can also support by enjoying music together, exposing your child to a wider variety of musical styles.

Expectations

Regular practice is essential in piano learning. Establishing good habits early makes a big difference, starting with 5–10 minutes a day for young beginners, and gradually building up to 20–30 minutes a day for elementary-level students. Parents can help by setting a consistent practice routine and reducing distractions.

Engaging Lessons

Depending on the student's age, learning style, and prior experience, lessons include a variety of engaging "off-the-piano" and "at-the-piano" exercises. Students may explore music through movement, imaginative storytelling, visual mediums, listening exercises, and interactive games. Students will remember their lessons as fun, and that they too could make beautiful music. There will also be short theory and composition assignments and weekly practice logs that help foster early habits.

Examinations, Competitions, and Performances

Music is meant to be shared. Students are encouraged to perform for their families and friends, participate in school or community events, or share performances on social media (with appropriate privacy settings).

Competitions, festivals, and examinations are great ways to set short-term goals for motivation and assessment. I encourage students to take part in competitions and festivals with the intent to gain performing experience and become more confident performing in front of other people. I will work with families to determine when an examination is appropriate for a student, and will prepare students in terms of solidifying techniques and fine-tuning their performance of their pieces to set them up for success.

I look forward to working with each student to grow their skills, confidence, and creativity, and to guide them through their lifelong journey of enjoying music.
